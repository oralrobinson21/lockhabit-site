Didn’t Get a W-2? Here’s How to File Your Taxes Anyway

Updated November 3, 2025 8 min read

It’s late February, everyone around you is talking about tax refunds, and you’re still waiting on a W-2 that never showed up. Maybe you moved. Maybe the company changed hands. Maybe payroll is just slow.

Whatever the reason, the IRS still expects you to report your income—even if the form never arrives. The good news is that you have options, and the process is clearer than it sounds.

Big picture: You’re allowed to file without a W-2 as long as you make a good-faith effort to get it and accurately report what you earned.

Step 1: Double-check that the W-2 is actually missing

Before you panic, check the usual places:

  • Your old work email (including spam/junk)
  • Any online payroll portals (ADP, Paychex, Workday, UKG, etc.)
  • Your physical mailbox at your current and previous address

Many employers post W-2s online and never mail them if you opted for electronic delivery.

Step 2: Contact your employer or old HR department

If you still can’t find it, reach out directly:

  • Call or email HR/payroll and confirm they have your current address.
  • Ask whether the W-2 was mailed or posted online.
  • Request a reprint or digital copy.

Make a note of the date you contacted them. If the company closed or you can’t reach anyone, that’s important to document too.

Step 3: Collect your own records as a backup

While you wait, start building your own file. Gather:

  • Last paystub of the year (shows year-to-date wages and taxes)
  • Any earlier paystubs if the last one is missing
  • Bank statements showing direct deposits

These numbers are what you’ll use if you end up filing without the W-2.

Use the W-2 Estimator & Paystub Rebuilder to turn your final paystub into an estimated W-2 with wages, Social Security, Medicare, and tax withheld.

Step 4: If the W-2 still doesn’t show up, use IRS Form 4852

When you’ve tried to get the form and it’s still not available, the IRS lets you substitute it with Form 4852, Substitute for Form W-2.

On Form 4852 you’ll enter:

  • Your best estimate of total wages
  • Federal income tax withheld
  • Social Security and Medicare withheld
  • How you calculated those numbers (for example: “Based on final paystub dated 12/29/2025”)

Most major tax software can walk you through this form step-by-step.

Step 5: File on time—don’t wait forever

The IRS generally expects W-2s to be sent by January 31. If it’s already mid-February and nothing has arrived, you don’t have to sit on your hands until April.

You can either:

  • Request a filing extension while you keep chasing the form, or
  • File using Form 4852 with your best estimates.

If later you get the real W-2 and the numbers are very different, you may need to file an amended return (Form 1040-X).

Will filing without a W-2 trigger an audit?

Using Form 4852 doesn’t automatically mean you’ll be audited. The IRS mainly wants to see that:

  • You made a real attempt to get your W-2.
  • Your numbers line up reasonably with what the employer eventually reports.

Keep copies of emails, letters, and your paystubs in case they ask how you came up with the amounts.

If your employer never reported the wages at all

Sometimes an employer is behind on their own filings. That’s a separate issue between them and the IRS—you should still report what you earned.

Reporting your income protects you. If the IRS eventually finds unreported wages and you didn’t claim them, they’ll come to you for the tax, penalties, and interest.

When to call the IRS directly

If it’s after February 14 and you still haven’t received your W-2 or a helpful response from your employer, the IRS sometimes suggests calling them for guidance. They may contact the employer or make a note in your file.

Quick recap: your game plan

  • Search online portals and email for the missing W-2.
  • Contact your old employer and request a corrected or reissued form.
  • Gather paystubs and bank records as backup.
  • Use a calculator to estimate wages and tax withheld.
  • File on time using Form 4852 if you still don’t have the W-2.

It’s frustrating when paperwork doesn’t show up, but you’re not stuck. With decent records and a little math, you can file on time and keep your relationship with the IRS boring—in the best way.
